## Review Criteria

### Keyword Match

Check whether the resume captures the important language of the JD.

Look for:

- Required technical skills, tools, frameworks, certifications, and methods.
- Role-specific nouns and phrases used by the employer.
- Seniority and responsibility signals.
- Natural placement of keywords in summary, skills, and experience.
- Avoidance of obvious keyword stuffing.

### Role Alignment

Check whether the resume makes the candidate look aligned to the target role.

Look for:

- Relevant responsibilities mapped to the JD.
- Matching domain, industry, product, or workflow context where available.
- Seniority level reflected accurately.
- Clear evidence for must-have qualifications.
- Strongest relevant experience placed early enough to be seen.

### Bullet Quality

Check whether experience bullets are persuasive and specific.

Look for:

- Action, scope, and result in each major bullet.
- Metrics, scale, frequency, latency, revenue, cost, adoption, quality, or time savings where available.
- Clear ownership and contribution.
- Concise phrasing without vague filler.
- Bullets that support the target role rather than generic work history.

### Skills Coverage

Check whether the resume covers required and preferred skills from the JD.

Look for:

- Must-have skills present in the skills section or experience.
- Preferred skills represented when the profile supports them.
- No unsupported skills added beyond the candidate evidence.
- Skills grouped clearly enough for ATS and recruiter scanning.
- Consistency between listed skills and experience bullets.

### ATS Parseability

Check whether the resume is likely to be parsed correctly by ATS systems.

Look for:

- Standard headings such as Summary, Skills, Experience, Education, Projects, and Certifications.
- Consistent employer, title, location, and date formatting.
- Text selectable in exported PDFs.
- Simple section hierarchy.
- No tables, columns, images, icons, headers, or footers that cause parse errors.
- Contact details readable as plain text.

### Recruiter Readability

Check whether a recruiter can understand fit quickly.

Look for:

- Clear target positioning in the top third of the resume.
- Strongest qualifications visible within a quick skim.
- Dense but readable bullets.
- No repetitive phrasing or inflated claims.
- Logical ordering of sections and roles.
- Clear distinction between core skills, tools, and outcomes.
